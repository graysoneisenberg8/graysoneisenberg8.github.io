Wildfire-Detecting Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) - 11th Grade

Designed, manufactured and coded a Wildfire-Detecting Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) during my Advanced Makerspace X class. For my year-long junior thesis project, I drew upon my previous experience researching and building drones to develop this quadcopter UAV to detect wildfires in remote areas.

Design & PlanningPrototypingFabrication & AssemblyTesting & Finished Product
01

Wildfire Detecting UAVDesign & Planning

My design process included using existing drones as a reference to determine dimensions, weight, function and material specifications. I sketched the design on paper before creating a cardboard model. I then planned out the design of the drone’s frame virtually on Adobe Illustrator. Finally, I created the model of the camera housing to be 3D printed.

02

Wildfire Detecting UAVPrototyping

During the prototype phase, I laser cut the design onto cardboard where I was able to rework and revise the design to make adjustments to the alignment of the screw holes, length of the arms, and tweaking the fit of the arms. Using the revised version, I laser-cut a wood model for a full prototype of the UAV. I then created a 3D model of the camera housing to be 3D printed, testing multiple iterations.

03

Wildfire Detecting UAVFabrication & Assembly

After the prototyping phase, I modeled my tested and completed design using Autodesk Fusion software. This allowed me to run my design through a metal CNC machine to cut the frame into carbon fiber. From this point, I assembled the drone using the frame parts I created, then installed and wired the Flight Controller (FC), Electronic Speed Controller (ESC), GPS module, motors, and propellers into a fully assembled UAV.

04

Wildfire Detecting UAVTesting & Finished Product

The next phase required coding and testing of the UAV. I programmed and coded the drone to fly autonomously using ArduPilot Mission Planner software. This phase required a lot of troubleshooting with revisions and adjustments to the coding as I ran into multiple hardware and software issues.

When the drone was completed, I then had to test and experiment with the UAV and continually modify it to account for a multitude of challenges. Once I solved for these issues, the drone was ready for detection testing, using a heat lamp placed outside in various locations.

0210th Grade

C TableWelding & Woodworking - 10th Grade

I created a C table in my Makerspace 2 class combining skills in metalworking and woodworking. First, I analyzed the ideal height and width of the table for use with a popular chair in our school’s production lab. Next, I sketched the design and dimensions first on paper. I took rectangular metal rods and cut them to size using a metal bandsaw, before welding them together and painting them. Using a table saw, I cut an oak wood slab to the desired dimensions and then sanded and stained it. To complete the C table, I secured the wood top to the metal base using J-B Weld epoxy. I donated this table to my school’s production lab where students have been using it every day.

0310th Grade

Push CartWelding Project - 10th Grade

For my Maker Space 2 welding project, I built a pushcart. The assignment was to practice our welding by making any object we wanted, and I chose a cart that holds a Jeep hardtop so it can be stored and moved around.

I started by measuring the hardtop and sketching the cart’s size and layout on paper. Then I cut the metal bars, welded them together, and smoothed out the welds into a base and a handle that also works as a side support. I added an angled, V-shaped bracket so the roof lays diagonally. I welded brackets to the bottom of the base and mounted casters on them so the cart rolls. Lastly, I padded the handle and the brackets to protect the hardtop.

First Person View DronePassion Project - 10th Grade

I built a First Person View (FPV) drone as a self-initiated passion project. I researched the materials I needed and where to procure them. I sketched out the design and, using online resources, I taught myself the methods to assemble the parts and how to operate the drone. This project was the basis from which I developed many skills, including wiring, soldering, Betaflight programming and piloting which I later applied to future projects.
